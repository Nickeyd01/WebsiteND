About

I am a PhD candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at UCLA, advised by Prof. Prineha Narang, expecting to defend in January 2027. My work sits on the seam between superconducting qubit hardware and quantum information theory: I design and measure circuit QED devices, then build the models that predict how those devices behave once they are connected to each other.

Two threads run through it. The first is nonreciprocity on chip — using superconducting diodes as a coherent, direction-dependent element for two-qubit gates. The second is what happens when you leave the fridge: transduction to optical photons, entanglement distillation across lossy channels, and routing protocols for distributing multipartite entanglement over a real network.

I have worked at IBM Quantum on optical links between superconducting processors, at AFRL, and most recently at Nokia Bell Labs on polarization-entangled photon distribution and teleportation over fiber at telecom wavelengths.

Before UCLA I completed a BSc in Electrical Engineering at ETH Zürich, where I worked on silicon nitride grating couplers for photonic integrated circuits, and an MSc in Physics at UCLA focused on mesoscopic optics and quantum electronics.

Publications & preprints

  1. 2026

    Probing time-reversal-symmetry breaking using a nonlinear superconducting ring resonator

    N. Dirnegger, M. Wesson, A. Arora, I. Petrides, J. B. Curtis, E. M. Been, A. Yacoby, P. Narang

    Editors’ Suggestion Phys. Rev. A 113, 062402 (2026) · Open access

    A multimode ring resonator whose own nonlinear cross-mode interactions act as a built-in amplifier. Driving a two-mode circuit with self- and cross-Kerr nonlinearities near the bifurcation threshold forces the photon populations into a symmetric configuration — which then breaks measurably under even weak time-reversal-symmetry breaking in a coupled quantum material.

    Garraway's pseudomode construction, generalized to nonlinearly intercoupled auxiliary modes, so that a dissipative environment around a Josephson nonlinearity can be reduced without hiding the approximation.

  3. 2025

    Nonreciprocal quantum information processing with superconducting diodes in circuit quantum electrodynamics

    N. Dirnegger, P. Narang, A. Arora

    Under review · npj Quantum Information arXiv:2511.20758

    An asymmetric SQUID, flux-biased into a superconducting diode, gives direction-dependent resonance shifts in transmission — and from there, coherent nonreciprocal qubit–qubit coupling.

  4. 2025

    N. Dirnegger, M. Malekakhlagh, V. Siddhu, A. Rao, C. Xiong, M. Kumph, J. Orcutt, A. Falk

    IBM Quantum arXiv:2503.10842

    Monte Carlo simulation mapping transducer device specs onto system-level channel performance. Extreme photon loss distillation performs best; today's transducers sit right at the 50% Bell-pair fidelity threshold.

  5. 2024

    R. Negrin, N. Dirnegger, W. Munizzi, J. Talukdar, P. Narang

    Under review · IEEE Trans. Quantum Engineering arXiv:2408.07118

Earlier work

2022

Efficient silicon nitride grating couplers

BSc thesis · Institute of Electromagnetic Fields, ETH Zürich

Supervised by Prof. Jürg Leuthold, Manuel Kohli and Boris Vukovic

Silicon nitride is an attractive platform for photonic integrated circuits — low propagation loss, CMOS compatible, tolerant of high input power — but getting light from a fiber into a micron-scale waveguide is the bottleneck. I demonstrated a SiN grating coupler with under 0.5 dB coupling loss using a back reflector, and a 40 nm 1 dB bandwidth using amorphous silicon overlay gratings. The work was later published by my supervisor Dr. Manuel Kohli and his team in ACS Photonics.

2018

Creating a GenEARator

Christina Henzinger & Nicolas Dirnegger · Vienna

First place, Vienna science fair

A secondary-school project: generating electricity from the heat emitted by human ears. We built Peltier tiles into the earpieces of a headset, with body heat on the inner face and cold air on the outer, and worked through which tile to use, how to stop the two sides thermalising, and whether to wire the tiles in series or parallel. It produced enough power to light an LED, and won the Vienna science fair in 2018. Kinder-KURIER covered it as “Strom aus der Wärme von Kopfhörern”. It is the earliest thing on this page, and not unrelated to the rest of it: a small temperature difference, carefully harvested.
